1 · One book

Six chapters become eighty

Calvin published the Institutes of the Christian Religion at Basel in 1536, a small book in six chapters. He rewrote it for twenty-three years. Scroll through the five Latin editions.

    The library holds the 1559 text in Henry Beveridge’s English. Open it at the first book.

    2 · The Reformed International

    A church of refugees and professors

    The Reformed churches had no single capital. Their teachers fled, were called, and moved on: Junius from Bourges to Geneva, Heidelberg and Leiden; Alsted from Herborn to Transylvania. Scroll to watch the academies open and the writers travel between them.

      3 · What the churches confessed

      A confession for every church

      Where the Lutherans gathered their confessions into one Book of Concord, each Reformed church wrote its own: cities, kingdoms, synods and refugee congregations. The library holds 127 of them in its Confessions room. Each small card is one, and the great ones are dealt as cards.

      4 · The method

      Divide, and divide again

      Peter Ramus taught that any subject is best laid out by dividing it in two, and each part in two again. The Reformed schools took to it. Amandus Polanus of Basel built his Syntagma of Christian Theology (1609) this way, and its first two books open with the whole tree. Scroll to follow it from theology to the two tables of the law; each branch opens at its chapter.

        5 · The generations

        Two hundred years of Reformed schools
